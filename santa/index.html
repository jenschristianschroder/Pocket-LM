    Delivery rules & teaching tips

    A chimney delivery takes 6 minutes and works only if the present fits. A front-door delivery takes 10 minutes and always fits. Each attempt uses 12% energy. Below 12% energy, delivery or travel fails and costs 6 minutes.

    Resting costs 12 minutes and restores 45% energy, up to 100%. Skipping costs 8 minutes and 6% energy; Santa visits the next waiting child and must return later. Weather is a model input but adds no extra time in this introductory challenge. Travel time is included in these action costs.

    Choose the chimney when the gift fits, use the door for larger gifts, and rest when energy is too low. Teach these choices in practice, then train. All presents must arrive strictly before sunrise. The clock measures simulated minutes, so you can pause and think.

      A tiny brain, with nothing up its sleeve +

      This is a real neural network: 5 inputs, 24 hidden neurons, 4 action scores, and 244 learned parameters. The starter weights were trained on 5,000 synthetic numeric situations. No stories or external datasets were used.

      The starter’s examples reflect a made-up delivery policy. Its decisions come from the trained weights; there are no delivery rules overriding them. Your Santa learns only from the examples you label. A small dataset can lead to surprising or poor decisions. In the challenge, transparent simulation rules determine action costs and whether a delivery succeeds. These rules never choose or override the model’s action. In free practice, animations only illustrate the choice.
